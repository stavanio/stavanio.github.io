04 / Garage

From the garage

No lab manager, no procurement queue, no one to hand the hard part to. Just a bench, a power supply, and machines that either work or do not.

What I am building

One closed loop, at home: a rover that perceives and maps, an arm that touches and manipulates, and edge compute that has to decide without the cloud.

It is a small-scale version of the problem every physical AI company runs into. Perception arrives late, wrong, or uncertain, and it feeds a machine that can break things. I am building and testing the safety layer that sits between the AI and the motors, on real hardware, where a bad command has a sound.

Rig 01 · Manipulation

B601 arm

A six-axis arm with a gripper that I brought up from a kit and drive with my own controller instead of a vendor black box.

200 Hz
Custom joint-level control loop
CAN bus
Seven motors, integrated and tuned by hand
48 V
Actuation bus on bench power

What it took

  • Brought every joint online over CAN, one motor at a time, before anything was allowed to move.
  • Chased an elbow that dropped with a thud down to the raw CAN traffic: the stock software was cutting torque once a second and letting gravity win.
  • Wrote gravity compensation so the arm holds itself up instead of fighting itself.

What it achieves

  • Soft grasp and lift under my own control code.
  • A manipulation and safety testbed I understand end to end, from CAN frame to fingertip.

Rig 02 · Autonomy

Yahboom rover

An omnidirectional rover that maps a room, finds its way around it, and shows exactly what happens when perception falls behind the world.

LiDAR
Mapping and localization
Mecanum
Omnidirectional drive for tight indoor courses
ROS
Sensor fusion, mapping, and navigation stack

What it took

  • Out of the box it skidded, mapped badly, and drove the wrong way. I found four root causes: a mirrored LiDAR scan, an IMU reporting zeros from a firmware mismatch, sensor fusion that never received IMU data, and a navigation config fighting the robot.
  • The test course is masking tape at half-meter marks, a tape measure, and a mug slid across the path.

What it achieves

  • Maps a living room and navigates it on its own, returning to its start point within about an inch.
  • Wheel odometry within 0.8% over two meters.
  • Runs the same path under controlled perception delays, showing how stale data erodes safety.

Setup and fixes, open source

    Jetson Orin nodes running ROS 2 and on-device inference.

  • Training

    An RTX 5080 workstation for training, simulation, and benchmark sweeps.

  • Bench

05 / Research

Physical AI research, built on real hardware

Alongside the industry work, I run an independent research program on one question: what makes a robot safe to deploy? It starts with perception, because most failures begin with a machine that is confidently wrong about the world, and it is moving toward manipulation. Six projects, open code, and real hardware in the loop.

  • ICRA 2026 workshop, spotlight

    Perception Debt

    A formal model of how small, individually tolerable perception errors accumulate and compound until a robot crosses a safety threshold.

    HowDefines a debt-to-margin ratio and a time-to-default estimate that a running robot can track.

    Why it mattersTurns “the camera is a bit off” into a measurable safety budget, with an estimate of how long a robot has before it fails.

    Code

  • Under review, IEEE T-RO

    SESR-Bench

    Clearance-aware evaluation of visual-inertial odometry.

    How7 estimators including ORB-SLAM3, BASALT, and OpenVINS; 6 sequences; 210 trajectories scored as error divided by clearance.

    Why it mattersFive centimeters of drift is fine in a warehouse aisle and fatal in a 12 cm gap. This scores localization against the free space a robot actually has.

    Code

  • Under review, Communications Engineering

    TRiX

    A neuro-symbolic runtime safety layer that checks policy- and VLM-generated robot commands against physics-derived limits before execution.

    HowAllow, project, or reject each command; evaluated on a benchmark of six contact-rich disassembly tasks.

    Why it mattersA general-purpose AI model can propose a plausible action with no idea whether it will strip a thread or crack a board. TRiX sits between the model and the motors.

    Code

  • Working paper

    LWSD-Bench

    A single diagnostic for how fast a robot’s world model goes stale as perception latency grows.

    HowRuns as a ROS 2 node beside the robot stack; latency-injection experiments on a live rover.

    Why it mattersShows why a bigger, slower model can make a robot less safe, not more.

    Code

  • Working paper

    OVAC

    Open-vocabulary detection and aerial-ground coordination for search-and-rescue scenarios.

    HowEvaluated on aerial datasets with reproducible trial scripts and a threshold sweep.

    Why it mattersLets a drone and a ground robot look for things they were never trained on, using models small enough to run onboard.

    Code

  • In progress

    Diagnostic Manipulation

    Force-based probing that lets an arm learn what an object is by touching it, not just looking at it.

    HowRunning on the B601 arm in the lab.

    Why it mattersRobots that handle unknown objects need to feel stiffness and structure before they commit to a grasp.
